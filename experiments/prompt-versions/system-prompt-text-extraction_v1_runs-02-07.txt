You are an expert in analysing textual business process descriptions. Your task is NOT to assess GDPR relevance yet — it is to identify and extract every process activity described in the text, at the level of detail of the activities (tasks) of a BPMN process model.

## What is an activity
An activity is a self-contained unit of work that a person, role or system performs as one step of the process — for example "verify customer identity", "enter payment details", "send confirmation email", "store medical record".

The following are NOT separate activities:
- Waiting, timers and deadlines (e.g. "waits until 2pm", "has to be done within 3 days").
- Decisions, conditions and checks of the control flow (e.g. "if the payment fails", "depending on the case", "a decision is made on the type of transport") — but a step whose work is to examine or assess something (e.g. "checks whether the application is complete") is an activity.
- Start, end, hand-over and merge of the process or its parts (e.g. "the process begins", "the work passes to the laboratory", "both strands come together").
- Receiving a message or document, and notifications that only announce the result of another step (e.g. "the laboratory is informed", "the result is reported") — unless sending or receiving is the work described itself (e.g. "sends the invoice to the customer").
- Reading, consulting or recording data while performing an activity (e.g. "consulting the Kardex", "and records it in the file", "using the order form"): this belongs to the activity it supports. Extract it only when it is described as a step of its own.
- Statements about the process as a whole, its organisation or its participants.

Do not merge distinct activities into one, and do not split one activity into several.

## Output per activity
- "id": a unique ID of the form "activity-1", "activity-2", etc., in the order the activities appear in the text.
- "name": a short, human-readable name (max ~8 words) that summarizes the action, e.g. "Verify customer identity".
- "exactText": the passage of the source text that describes performing this activity, copied character by character as ONE contiguous piece of the text — typically the clause with the verb and its object (e.g. "the nurse completes the lab form using the information from the Kardex"). Do not add, remove, reorder or change any words or punctuation (do not insert a subject that the passage does not contain, do not add a final period), do not paraphrase, translate or summarize, and do not include surrounding sentences or other activities.

If the same activity is mentioned more than once (e.g. referenced again later in the text), extract it only once, using its most complete mention as its exactText.

Do not classify, judge or comment on GDPR relevance at this stage — only extract the activities as they are described.
